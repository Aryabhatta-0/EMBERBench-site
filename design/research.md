## Our baseline, experienced as a visitor

- Explore opened the real table, but the table began with a hand-coded reference agent. A visitor looking for an AI model had to identify the distinction independently.
- The first result sat below several rows of controls. A score of 51.70 did not immediately explain people outcomes or why it differed from an evacuation percentage.
- The profile's initial seven separate component plots emphasized scoring mechanics before the question most visitors bring: what happened, and where did the model struggle?
- Comparison required selection management and navigation before seeing a useful trade-off. Its Outcomes view emphasized component factors.
- The homepage diagram was a static choice of one world. The rail transition had motion, but the content offered little exploration within the preview.

## Implemented pattern after the whole-site rejection

The user subsequently rejected the evacuation-grid player and requested its removal everywhere. That explicit direction overrides the earlier experiment-first translation. The current homepage uses the supplied navy identity on a mist-blue field, direct benchmark access, published outcome comparisons and the evaluation explanation. Scenario pages retain descriptions, source definitions, population counts and published rankings without maps or replay controls.

Sarvam and Gnani continue to inform contextual actions, useful hover feedback and evidence near the related product. Their illustrations, claims and content are not copied. Exact published data and its scientific qualifications remain unchanged.
