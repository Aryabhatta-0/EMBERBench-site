This document is named "evaluation suite", not "held-out suite", on purpose: the leak guard (emberbench.leakguard) rejects any repository path with a heldout- component, so that private material cannot be committed by accident. The document holds no private scenario, seed or result. Every number comes from public development seeds.

0. Summary

  • One design: 10 primary families x 16 scenarios = 160 scenarios, 240 unique worlds. Per family: Easy 2, Moderate 4, Hard 6, Stress 4; 12 deterministic-fire scenarios (1 world each) and 4 stochastic-fire scenarios (3 distinct fire worlds each).
  • World identity: environment_world_digest, a seed-free digest of the scenario and its burning trajectory. The public suite is 640 nominal episodes but only 73 unique worlds (88.6% duplicates).
  • Difficulty: bands from the best official score B of a frozen four-baseline calibration panel: Easy B >= 80, Moderate [30, 80), Hard [10, 30), Stress < 10. A capacity filter (M_cap, official score_evidence) excludes worlds that are hard only because M* ignores exit and cut capacity.
  • Model trials: K = 1 for a verified-deterministic model, K = 3 for every other model, on the same frozen worlds: 240 or 720 episodes.
  • Precision: within-suite 95% CI about 7.9 points wide (about +-3.9); paired 95% width between adjacent comparators at most 5.8 points; comparator pairs more than 1.12 points apart keep their order in at least 95% of simulated suites.
  • Candidate pool: about 4,000 candidates (25x the suite) with the current recipes plus the probe recipes, then targeted top-up batches for any shortfall cell. The earlier "10x" figure is not supported: 1,120 candidates (7x) filled 87.6% of the quotas and never all of them.
  • Generator gap: the generator names 3 primary families. Nine of the ten primary families need some generator work (section 6): new proxies or constraints for three, fire-dependent evaluator-side tags for two, labels or recipes for four. Until then the evaluator assigns families by measured tags.

1. Literature review

Source Finding Implication for EMBERBench
Cobbe, Hesse, Hilton, Schulman. Leveraging Procedural Generation to Benchmark RL (Procgen), ICML 2020, arXiv:1912.01588 16 procedurally generated environments. Train on a finite level set, test on the full distribution; agents need many thousands of levels to close the generalization gap. Scores are normalized per environment by R_min and R_max. A fixed public suite of 10 scenarios is memorizable. Scored evaluation must draw from a procedural distribution that no model saw. Per-world normalization by a privileged reference (EMBER's M*) is the analogue of R_max; random-legal-v1 is the analogue of R_min.
Cobbe, Klimov, Hesse, Kim, Schulman. Quantifying Generalization in RL (CoinRun), ICML 2019, arXiv:1812.02341 Agents overfit to surprisingly large training-level sets. The number of distinct worlds, not the number of episodes, is the unit of evidence.
Machado et al. Revisiting the Arcade Learning Environment, JAIR 2018, arXiv:1709.06009 A deterministic environment lets an agent memorize an open-loop action sequence; sticky actions break it. Separate tuning games from reporting games. Deterministic worlds are memorizable when public. Repeating a deterministic world with a deterministic policy adds no evidence. Calibration and reporting use different worlds.
Agarwal et al. Deep RL at the Edge of the Statistical Precipice, NeurIPS 2021, arXiv:2108.13264 With few runs, point estimates mislead. Use stratified intervals, robust aggregates and the probability of improvement. Stratify by family. A stratum with one unit has no variance estimate. Every family needs several independent scenarios.
Henderson et al. Deep RL that Matters, AAAI 2018, arXiv:1709.06560 Two groups of 5 seeds of one algorithm can differ significantly. Seeds are a variance source to control and freeze, not a free sample-size multiplier.
Colas, Sigaud, Oudeyer. How Many Random Seeds?, 2018, arXiv:1806.08295 Power analysis for RL comparisons; small-sample tests are anti-conservative. Size the suite from a precision target, with margin, not from cost.
Bouthillier et al. Accounting for Variance in Machine Learning Benchmarks, MLSys 2021, arXiv:2103.03098 Data sampling is the largest variance source. In EMBER the analogue is which worlds are drawn. The study measures suite-sampling variance directly (section 11).
Miller. Adding Error Bars to Evals, 2024, arXiv:2411.00640 Clustered standard errors when items are grouped; K resamples per item cut within-item variance to sigma^2/K; paired differences. Worlds of one scenario are a cluster. K model trials on the same frozen worlds. Paired comparison on one pack.
Ray, Achiam, Amodei. Safety Gym, 2019; Ji et al. Safety-Gymnasium, 2023, arXiv:2310.12567 Difficulty levels by hazard count. EMBER needs evidence that its proxies track difficulty. They do not (difficulty-calibration.md, section 3): obstacle density correlates negatively.
Mattson et al. MLPerf Training, MLSys 2020, arXiv:1910.01500; Reddi et al. MLPerf Inference, ISCA 2020, arXiv:1911.02549 Run counts from measured variance; frozen rules per round; stated statistical confidence. Choose K and worlds per stochastic scenario from measured variance. Freeze rules and suite per round.
Liang et al. HELM, TMLR 2023, arXiv:2211.09110 Every model on the same instances; repeated trials for model randomness. One frozen pack for all models; model randomness by trials, not by new worlds.
Recht et al. Do ImageNet Classifiers Generalize to ImageNet?, ICML 2019, arXiv:1902.10811 A new test set from the same process changes absolute accuracy but mostly keeps the ranking. Scores are comparable only inside one suite revision.
Blum, Hardt. The Ladder, ICML 2015; Dwork et al. The Reusable Holdout, Science 2015 Repeated adaptive queries overfit a holdout. Limit scored submissions per suite; no per-world results while a suite is live.
Kiela et al. Dynabench, NAACL 2021; Ott et al., Nature Communications 2022 Static benchmarks saturate. Define saturation triggers in advance (section 16).
Jacovi et al. Stop Uploading Test Data in Plain Text, EMNLP 2023, arXiv:2305.10160 Public plain-text test data contaminates later models. Never publish a live pack. Publish digests only.
Zhang et al. GSM1k, 2024, arXiv:2405.00332 Some models drop on a fresh equivalent test set. A fresh suite from the same frozen rules is the contamination check (section 15).
White et al. LiveBench, 2024; Jain et al. LiveCodeBench, 2024 Dated, refreshed releases limit contamination. Each round gets a new suite identity; old suites are retired, not edited.
Rodriguez et al., ACL 2021; Polo et al. tinyBenchmarks, ICML 2024 Items differ in difficulty and discrimination. Prefer worlds that separate methods (admission rule). EMBER scores are bimodal, so a tiny suite is not enough.
Dennis et al. PAIRED, NeurIPS 2020, arXiv:2012.02096 Environment difficulty as regret against a strong reference. Band = score kept by the best fair reference heuristic (difficulty-calibration.md).
Kirk et al. A Survey of Zero-Shot Generalisation in Deep RL, JAIR 2023 Train and test contexts disjoint and drawn by a stated process. Development (public) and evaluation (private) seeds come from disjoint domains.
Card et al. With Little Power Comes Great Responsibility, EMNLP 2020 Many test sets are too small for the effects they report. State the smallest difference the suite resolves (section 11).

3. World identity and unique-world scheduling

In V1 the episode seed reaches a run only through the simulator's fire stream. The fire ignores the agents' actions, and the observation contains no seed or RNG state. A policy's view of an episode is therefore fixed by the scenario and the burning trajectory:

Environment world = (simulator version, observation version, scenario content hash, burning cells at every snapshot 0..H).

environment_world_digest = sha256(canonical_json({
    environment_world_version: "1.0", simulator_version, observation_version,
    scenario_hash, burning: [sorted [row, col] per snapshot 0..H]}))
  • The seed is not an input. Two seeds with equal trajectories are one world; a deterministic-fire scenario is one world for every seed.
  • The model is not an input. Tests check that every baseline sees exactly the precomputed trajectory.
  • PR #32's fire_world_digest is unchanged. It hashes the seed with the trajectory and stays the per-episode pack attribute. It is not used for deduplication.
  • build_unique_world_manifest(suite, candidate_seeds, *, stochastic_worlds) schedules each deterministic scenario once (the first candidate seed) and each stochastic scenario with the first stochastic_worlds candidate seeds of pairwise-different worlds. It raises EvaluationError when the candidates give fewer worlds. The result is a normal, validated EvaluationManifest; it freezes into an EnvironmentPack unchanged. It takes no model input. build_evaluation_manifest is unchanged.
  • Every count in this document is a count of unique worlds. Seed duplicates are never counted as evidence.

4. Families: audit and final list

Audit criteria (the brief's five): V1-supported (needs only V1 mechanics: grid, walls, exits, people, deterministic or stochastic fire), independently meaningful (tests a distinct decision capability), non-redundant (tag overlap, Jaccard), scoreable (admitted scenarios exist and the panel separates on them), generatable (the current generator produces it without hand-authoring). No V2 concept (smoke, psychology, floors, communication) is used.

5. Difficulty

The full rule and its evidence are in difficulty-calibration.md. In short:

  • Frozen calibration panel 1.0: nearest-exit-greedy-v1, static-shortest-path-v1, fire-aware-shortest-path-v1, congestion-aware-v1. random-legal-v1 is the floor reference only. The privileged reference is a bound only.
  • B = best panel mean official score over the scenario's worlds. Easy B >= 80 (best member has no avoidable loss), Moderate [30, 80) (about one), Hard [10, 30) (about two), Stress < 10 (three or more).
  • Admission, in order: not applicable; capacity artifact (mean capacity ceiling < 50); saturated (every member >= 95); floor (every member < 1); no separation (spread < 1 point, the official practical difference).
  • Development pool: 55.7% admitted. Structural proxies do not predict difficulty (largest |rho| 0.48, and negative for obstacle density).
  • Not circular: no model under evaluation is an input; thresholds are fixed numbers with a meaning on the score scale; rank stability is checked with two non-panel comparators.

6. Generator Coverage Gap

The current generator (version 1.0.0) produces scenarios from a config (grid, population, exits, ignitions, partitions, doors, door width, scatter, fire model, spread interval and probability, flammable exits) and accepts or rejects them by structural constraints (route length, obstacle density, crowd density, exit separation, passage width, route redundancy, ignition-person and ignition-exit distance). Its describe_families names only three primary families (single_exit_low_density, single_exit_high_density, split_flow_across_exits; every multi-exit scenario becomes split_flow_across_exits) and four secondary labels. It cannot target a difficulty band.

Evidence: the development pool (10 recipes, 1,120 scenarios) and the gap-probe command (four config-only recipes, development seeds; results in suite-design-results.md, part C).

Cross-cutting work for the next generator PR:

  1. A family-assignment rule for all ten primary families (today: three), with the thresholds frozen in a generator version.
  2. Recipes that target cells: a medium-population split-flow recipe, a crowded-maze recipe and stochastic variants of the dense, maze and crowd recipes. The probe shows that the last three need configuration only.
  3. Fire-dependent tags (threatened exit, route invalidation) cannot be a seed-free generator property for stochastic fire. They stay evaluator-side, measured on the selected unique worlds, and the freeze record stores them.
  4. Difficulty cannot be targeted by the generator. Bands come from the evaluator's calibration (section 5) and rejection sampling.

Until this work lands, the V1 suite is composed by evaluator-side tagging and rejection sampling from a large pool (section 12). This works for every cell (shown by the pool and the probe), but it is not yet a supported library feature. The design does not claim that the generator alone produces the 10-family suite.

(Simulated suites have fewer worlds than the design because four families have no stochastic candidate in the development pool and fall back to deterministic slots; with the probe recipes the design has 24 worlds per family.)

Decision: 16 per family.

  • 16 is the smallest size with a paired width of about 6 points or less (5.7 to 6.2 in independent simulations; half-width about 3 points) for every adjacent comparator pair; 10 and 12 are above 7.
  • It gives every family at least 4 scenarios in each of Moderate, Hard and Stress and 2 Easy controls, so every family x band cell has more than one unit.
  • 20 per family narrows the widths by about 5% to 13% for 25% more episodes, API calls and rare-cell candidates. That is not worth its cost in V1.
  • The "largest gap reversing" metric depends on which comparator pairs exist, so it is not monotone in size; at 16 every pair more than 1.12 points apart kept its order in at least 95% of suites.
  • Family coverage: all 10 families x 4 bands at every size from 10.

Why 2/4/6/4 is scientifically useful:

  • Headroom. The best V1 comparator scores about 29 points. A strong model has 70 points of room before saturation.
  • Not a floor suite. 22.4% of comparator x world scores are below 1 point, against 27.5% for the stress-heavy mix. Stress worlds give headroom, but every Stress world scores about 0 for weak policies and carries no information about them.
  • Separation. Moderate and Hard are where the panel spreads most (mean spread 36 and 15 points). The final mix has the most Moderate slots of the separating mixes. It matches the previous 2/4/8/6 in resolving power with fewer floor scores.
  • Controls. Two Easy worlds per family detect basic faults (a model that fails an Easy world has a bug, not a weakness). "No easy" removes them for no gain.
  • The stress-heavy mix resolves every counted pair only because three of its five adjacent pairs fall below the 1-point practical difference there and are not counted; it is not more precise.

9. Deterministic and stochastic fire

Variance components over admitted stochastic units (up to 6 worlds each): the world-to-world share of the variance is 24% to 42% depending on the comparator. So for a fixed episode budget, more scenarios always beat more worlds per scenario (Var = sigma_b^2 / S + sigma_w^2 / (S W)); extra worlds are worth their cost only to measure robustness to fire realisations inside a scenario.

(Simulated, 400 suites each; differences below about 0.5 points are Monte Carlo noise.)

Decision: 12 deterministic + 4 stochastic scenarios per family, 3 unique fire worlds per stochastic scenario.

  • Precision barely depends on the mix; the decision rests on coverage. Four stochastic scenarios per family (25% of scenarios, 50% of worlds) test robustness to fire randomness in every family, and 3 worlds give each one a within-scenario spread (the lower-tail CVaR10 width falls from 0.40 at 1 world to 0.33 at 3; 5 worlds add little).
  • More stochastic scenarios need stochastic candidates in every family. The development pool has none for four families (fallback 17% to 43%); the probe shows they need recipes only.
  • The previous 14/6 x 3 out of 20 is not kept: at 16 per family, 4 x 3 gives the same precision with fewer rare stochastic candidates.
  • Worlds come from build_unique_world_manifest: three seeds with pairwise different trajectories, never duplicates.

10. Model trials (K)

Model randomness is separate from environment randomness: trials repeat the same frozen worlds. Surrogate: congestion-aware-v1 with ε random actions, 10 trials on 60 admitted worlds; trial noise of the suite mean for 240 worlds.

Decision: K = 3 for every model that is not verified deterministic; K = 1 for a verified-deterministic model (two runs on the first 10 worlds give identical action sequences; otherwise K = 3). K = 3 removes about 70% to 75% of the possible gain; K = 5 adds 1 to 2 percentage points for 67% more calls. Re-check with the measured trial variance of the first real models: if the within-world share of a model's variance is above 0.5, use K = 5 for it.

P(every adjacent pair >= 1 point apart in the right order) 0.63

  • Overall score: about +-4 points. Two models whose paired difference is more than about 3 points are resolved. Differences near the official practical difference (1 point) are not: the two comparator pairs about 1.1 points apart reverse in about 20% of suites. Reports must call such pairs ties, not rank them.
  • Family scores are coarse (about +-7 points with 16 scenarios). Report them with intervals; never rank models on one family.
  • Lower tail: p10 and CVaR10 are 0 for every V1 comparator (the worst 10% of worlds are lost by every heuristic), so the lower-tail statistics are stable but carry information only for strong models.
  • Use paired differences on the same pack for model comparison.

Per family: 3.1% (crowd_100) to 17.9% (narrow_corridor_bottleneck). Rarest cells (current mix): maze_obstacle_routing/Moderate (0 hits), crowd_100/Moderate, deceptive_nearest_exit/Stress and maze_obstacle_routing/Stress (0.36%), the deceptive and maze Hard cells (0.45%).

Binomial requirement per cell at 99.875% (a 95% family-wise guarantee over 40 cells, Bonferroni): 3,611 candidates (22.6x the suite) for all cells except maze Moderate, which the current mix never produced. The probe's maze recipes produce it at 10% (crowded maze) and 20% (stochastic maze), so a targeted batch of 59 to 123 candidates fills it. The real selector filled 30%, 49%, 69% and 88% of the quotas from 140, 280, 560 and 1,120 candidates, and never all of them.

Decision: generate 4,000 candidates (25x the suite; 11% margin over the binomial requirement) with the current recipes plus the probe recipes. Then, for every cell with a shortfall, generate targeted batches of 200 with the best recipe for that cell until the cell is full or 10,000 candidates in total are reached. The stopping rule depends only on cell counts, never on a model. A cell still short at the cap is reported in the freeze record. The binomial model ignores competition between cells (one candidate serves one cell), so the empirical fill, not the binomial number, is the reason for the top-up rule. Evaluator cost: about 6.4 CPU hours for the panel on 4,000 candidates (1.79 h for 1,120).

14. Hidden evaluation protocol

In this order. No step uses any model under evaluation, and there is no model-specific regeneration.

  1. Freeze versions. Benchmark, simulator, observation, scoring (SCORING_VERSION), generator and generator-config versions; calibration panel 1.0; environment-world version 1.0; analysis tool version. Publish a hash of this rule set.
  2. Freeze quotas. 10 families x 16 (2/4/6/4), 4 stochastic x 3 worlds, admission rule, band thresholds, tag thresholds, pool size and top-up rule. Include them in the rule-set hash.
  3. Choose private seeds. From a CSPRNG in the evaluator's private workspace: one high-entropy generator seed per recipe, one evaluation-seed stream, one selection key. Publish SHA-256 commitments only.
  4. Generate the candidate pool (section 12). Drop duplicate scenario hashes and any scenario equal to a public or earlier-round scenario.
  5. Run the fixed calibration panel on every candidate's unique worlds (world_seeds_for, the same walk as build_unique_world_manifest, 3 worlds for stochastic fire): official EMBER Score, M*, M_cap, capacity ceiling, admission, band, tags.
  6. Stratify admitted candidates by family x band x fire model.
  7. Select deterministically with select_suite and the private key (steps 4 to 7 with top-up: emberbench.composition.compose_pool). Report every shortfall; never borrow another band.
  8. Freeze the unique evaluator worlds with build_unique_world_manifest (1 world per deterministic scenario, 3 per stochastic one). Check that every environment_world_digest is unique. In the library: compose_suite, freeze_composed_suite (suite directory + private suite-freeze.json) and schedule_composed_worlds, which also checks every scheduled world against the frozen calibrated world.
  9. Create one immutable EnvironmentPack (freeze_environment_pack) with a new suite ID, version and batch ID.
  10. Commit only digests publicly before evaluation: pack digest, suite hash, manifest hash, rule-set hash, counts per family x band x fire model. Seeds, key, scenarios and measurements stay in the private freeze record.
  11. Evaluate every model on the same pack, with its K, replay-verified. Record provider, model ID and version, date and K for every run.

15. Leakage and contamination

  • No hidden material to developers. They get the public suite, the generator, the rules and the commitments. They never get hidden scenarios, seeds, world digests, bands or per-world results of a live suite. The observation contains no seed.
  • No per-world results while a suite is live. Publish overall and per-family scores with intervals only.
  • Provider and model identity are recorded per run (section 14, step 11). A suite is treated as exposed to every provider that served it.
  • No hidden suite in release artifacts. Private material lives only under leak-guarded paths; tools/check_public_assets.py checks the built distribution; tests check that no study cache or generated suite is committed and that the tool and worlds.py contain no seed literal.
  • Reveal only after retirement: generator seeds, selection key, pack and freeze record, so that anybody can check the suite with verify_regeneration.
  • Canary policy. A canary string in prompts or archives can show, after the fact, that a copy of the material appeared somewhere it was searched for (a crawl, a dataset, a model output that reproduces it verbatim). It cannot show that a model was trained on the suite, and its absence proves nothing. EMBER observations are grid states, which a model can learn from without reproducing any string. This design therefore does not use a canary as a contamination test. A harness may add one to pack archives for provenance tracing only, and must describe it that way.
  • Contamination check (when required). Before a round closes, if a model is suspected (for example a large jump between rounds), generate a fresh check suite with the same frozen rules, a new seed and a new key, and run that model and the calibration panel on it. A model whose paired score (against the panel) on the live suite exceeds its paired score on the check suite by more than the paired 95% interval is flagged for review. This is a statistical check of overfitting to the live suite, not proof of training contamination.

16. Refresh and version policy

A live suite is replaced, never edited, and never replaced silently. Every replacement has a new suite ID and version, a new pack digest and a published reason. Scores on different suites are not pooled or ranked together. The scoring version is separate from the suite version: a suite records the scoring version it was calibrated with, and a scoring change does not rename a suite by itself.

Trigger Action
Leak of hidden material Retire at once; new suite; re-run affected models
Contamination flag confirmed (section 15) Retire; new suite
Saturation: the best model scores >= 90 overall or on Stress New calibration-panel version with a stronger reference; new suite
Generator bug that affects selected worlds Retire; fix; new generator version; new suite
Scoring or benchmark revision (SCORING_VERSION or rule change) Re-calibrate the bands on the new score; new suite
Simulator or observation revision New suite (world digests change)
Invalid hidden world (for example a wrong M* or a capacity artifact found later) Retire the suite and publish the defect; never drop a world from a live suite
Scheduled New suite each evaluation round (at least every 6 months); retire and reveal the previous one

To compare rounds, run the calibration panel on every suite and report models relative to it (a linking set), not raw scores across suites.
