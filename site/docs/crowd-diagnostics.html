Crowd coordination diagnostics (crowd diagnostics version 1.0)

This document defines every field returned by emberbench.compute_crowd_diagnostics(scenario, record) -> CrowdDiagnostics. The code is in src/emberbench/crowd.py. The tests are in tests/test_crowd.py.

The diagnostics make congestion and bottlenecks measurable. They observe a recorded episode. They do not change the V1 movement rules, and they do not model human behaviour. There is no crowd score.

The document has four parts, kept apart on purpose:

  1. Simulator crowd rules: the frozen V1 rules that produce the data.
  2. Raw observed signals: counts read from the recorded movements and events.
  3. Proxies: values that use an assumption, named as proxies.
  4. What EMBERBench does not model.

1. Simulator crowd rules

These rules are frozen in V1. They are defined in simulation-contract.md and v1-fairness-spec.md. They are repeated here only so that the signals below make sense. Phase 7 changes none of them.

  • People are homogeneous. Each person occupies one cell and moves at most one cell (4-neighbour) per step.
  • One person per cell after every step.
  • A move into a cell held by a person who does not move gives blocked_occupied. A chain that ends at such a person is also blocked_occupied all the way back.
  • When several movers target the same cell, the lowest person ID (string order) wins. The others get blocked_conflict.
  • A closed chain (a swap or a longer rotation) is never allowed. Every member gets blocked_cycle.
  • A person may follow another person into the cell that person leaves in the same step, so a queue advances without gaps.
  • An exit is one cell. A person who ends a step on a non-burning exit evacuates. So one exit cell evacuates at most one person per step, and k distinct exit cells evacuate at most k people per step.

The six blocked reasons are canonical: boundary, wall and fire (static blocks, from the map or the fire), and occupied, conflict and cycle (congestion blocks, from other people).

2. Inputs, validation and versions

Item Value
Inputs a Scenario and a complete RunRecord of that scenario
Validation emberbench.validate_run_record, the same three stages as the metrics: compatibility, structural checks, and full simulator replay (verify_record)
Failure MetricsInputError; no partial result
Result an immutable (frozen) CrowdDiagnostics
Artifact version CROWD_DIAGNOSTICS_VERSION = "1.0", separate from METRICS_VERSION
Binding the result holds the simulator, observation and schema versions, scenario_id, scenario_hash, seed and record_digest (RunRecord.digest())

A record whose block reasons were relabelled (for example blocked_conflict changed to blocked_occupied) still passes the structural checks. The replay stage rejects it. So every count below is a count of what the simulator really did.

3. Raw observed signals

3.1 Contenders

A contending move is a movement attempt (an action that is not stay) whose outcome is not blocked_boundary, blocked_wall or blocked_fire. It passed the static checks, so it ends as moved, blocked_occupied, blocked_conflict or blocked_cycle.

The contenders for a cell c at step t are the contending moves of step t whose recorded target is c. A cell is contested at step t when it has two or more contenders. Two movers that target a cell held by a person who stays are contenders too: they both want the same cell.

A swap is not contention: each member targets a different cell.

3.2 Step signals (steps)

One entry per recorded step, in step order. The counts cover the people active when the step was decided.

Field Definition
step the step number t
active_people decisions in the step (one per active person)
stays outcome stayed
movement_attempts active_people - stays
successful_moves outcome moved
blocked_moves outcome blocked_*
blocked_by_outcome count per blocked outcome, in MoveOutcome order
congestion_blocks blocked_occupied + blocked_conflict + blocked_cycle
contending_moves movement_attempts - (blocked_boundary + blocked_wall + blocked_fire)
contested_cells cells with two or more contenders in this step
max_contenders the largest contender count of one cell in this step; 0 if there are no contending moves
evacuations_by_exit evacuations in this step, per exit (every exit, sorted by exit ID)
cumulative_evacuations_by_exit evacuations up to and including this step, per exit
nearest_exit_demand_proxy see section 4

Checked: successful_moves + blocked_moves = movement_attempts.

3.3 Spatial congestion hotspots (cells)

One entry per cell that had at least one contender in the episode. The entries are in canonical row-major order ((row, col) ascending). Static blocks are not counted here, so wall and boundary cells never appear.

Field Definition
cell [row, col]
exit_id the exit on this cell, or null
static_exit_distance wall-only path distance from this cell to the nearest exit (section 4), or null
attempted_entries contenders for this cell, summed over all steps
successful_entries contenders with outcome moved
blocked_occupied contenders with outcome blocked_occupied
blocked_conflict contenders with outcome blocked_conflict
blocked_cycle contenders with outcome blocked_cycle
congestion_blocks blocked_occupied + blocked_conflict + blocked_cycle
contention_steps steps in which the cell had two or more contenders
peak_contenders the largest contender count of this cell in one step

Checked: attempted_entries = successful_entries + congestion_blocks, and at most one contender enters a cell per step.

A hotspot is a cell with congestion_blocks > 0. The API returns the raw table. It does not rank cells. To find the worst hotspots, sort by congestion_blocks (or by contention_steps) and break ties by cell.

Hotspots are observed runtime hotspots of one episode under one decision model. They are not claims about where real crowds congest.

3.4 Exit throughput (exits)

Per exit (exits.exits, sorted by exit ID):

Field Definition
exit_id, position the exit
evacuations people who evacuated through this exit
peak_step_evacuations most evacuations through this exit in one step; at most 1 under V1
steps_with_evacuation steps with at least one evacuation here; equals evacuations under V1
first_evacuation_step, last_evacuation_step or null if unused
initial_demand_proxy see section 4
demand_proxy_person_steps see section 4

Episode level: exit_count, total_evacuations, and peak_step_evacuations (most evacuations over all exits in one step).

Cumulative throughput per step is in steps[t].cumulative_evacuations_by_exit.

3.5 Episode totals (totals)

Field Definition
decisions, stays, movement_attempts, successful_moves, blocked_moves, blocked_by_outcome, congestion_blocks, contending_moves sums of the step fields
contested_cell_steps sum of contested_cells over the steps
steps_with_contention steps with contested_cells > 0
steps_with_congestion_blocks steps with congestion_blocks > 0
peak_contested_cells, peak_contenders, peak_congestion_blocks maxima over the steps
hotspot_cells cells with congestion_blocks > 0

4. Proxy: the static nearest-exit demand

The nearest-exit demand proxy counts active people by the exit that is nearest to them on the static map. It is a proxy. It does not observe which exit a decision model intends to use, and it does not say which exit a person should use.

Distance is the static wall-only path distance: the length of the shortest 4-neighbour path from the cell to the exit that does not cross a wall or leave the grid. People, fire (also the initial ignitions) and the other exits are ignored. It is computed once per exit by breadth-first search. It is not Manhattan distance: a wall can make a close exit far.

For each active person:

  • if one exit is strictly nearer than every other exit, the person counts for that exit;
  • if two or more exits share the smallest distance, the person counts as tied;
  • if no exit is reachable around the walls, the person counts as unreachable.

So sum(per_exit) + tied + unreachable = active people.

Field Definition
steps[t].nearest_exit_demand_proxy per_exit, tied, unreachable for the people active when step t was decided
exits.exits[i].initial_demand_proxy the per-exit count at step 0 (the whole population)
exits.exits[i].demand_proxy_person_steps the per-exit count summed over all steps
exits.initial_demand_tied, exits.initial_demand_unreachable the step-0 counts
exits.demand_tied_person_steps, exits.demand_unreachable_person_steps summed over all steps

Comparing the proxy with the measured evacuations shows how the actual load differs from the static nearest-exit load. A difference is not an error. Fire, congestion and queue length can make another exit the better choice. Equal load across exits is not assumed to be optimal, and nothing here rewards it.

5. Determinism and serialisation

  • Every value is an integer, a cell or null. There is no floating point.
  • Steps are in step order, cells in row-major order, exits in exit-ID order. to_json() is canonical JSON (sorted keys, no spaces), and digest() is its SHA-256.
  • The result does not depend on the insertion order of the action mappings, on PYTHONHASHSEED, or on the platform. The tests check repeated runs, shuffled and reversed action maps, three hash seeds in subprocesses, and pinned golden digests (run on Linux, Windows and macOS in CI).

6. Tested queue and throughput invariants

tests/test_crowd.py pins these frozen V1 rules through the diagnostics:

  • one exit cell evacuates at most one person per step;
  • two distinct exit cells can evacuate two people in one step;
  • a following chain advances without gaps;
  • same-target losers are blocked_conflict;
  • movers into a person who does not move are blocked_occupied;
  • swaps and rotations are blocked_cycle;
  • step signals reconcile exactly with the recorded movements (an independent recomputation);
  • episode totals reconcile exactly with MovementMetrics and ExitUtilization from compute_episode_metrics.

Large-population stress tests run 50, 100 and 200 people in a synthetic room (crowd_stress_scenario in tests/conftest.py). They check movement resolution (order independence, one person per cell), simulator execution, replay authentication and the diagnostics. They use bounded step counts and exact pinned results, not wall-clock limits. For manual timing, run python tools/profile_crowd.py; it is not a CI gate.

7. What EMBERBench does not model

The simulator is a discrete grid with homogeneous agents. It does not model, and the diagnostics do not measure:

  • panic, stress or any other emotional state;
  • pushing force, crowd pressure or crush;
  • body size, shape or the space a person takes beyond one cell;
  • group behaviour (families, following a leader, herding);
  • social dynamics (helping, yielding, competition, norms);
  • heterogeneous speeds, mobility or reaction times.

The signals describe how one decision model's actions interact under the V1 rules. They are useful to compare models on the same episode. They are not evidence about real human evacuations.
