EMBERBench scientific evaluation methodology

This document explains why the EMBER Score (scoring version 1.0) has its form. The normative definition is scoring.md. The frozen environment protocol is environment-pack.md. The full output of the sensitivity study is scoring-study-results.md.

Nothing here changes the simulator, the observation, the schemas or the metrics. The score is computed only from authenticated, replayed run records.

1. Summary of decisions

Question Decision
Form EMBER = 100 * Safety * Quality^beta * Severity; every factor in [0, 1] (design F of section 5.9)
Safety E^2 * P_casualty * P_stranding, with a discrete incident factor plus a rate term for each loss type, and a catastrophic gate at half the savable people
Attribution per person: a loss is avoidable when that person's privileged earliest escape exists; unavoidable losses are never transferred between people
Quality weighted geometric mean of Time, Hazard, Path and Replanning (weights 3/8, 2/8, 1/8, 2/8), raised to the derived exponent beta = ln 0.6 / ln 0.5
Trajectory invariance every score input is a function of the authenticated state trajectory; identical trajectories score identically (R16)
Congestion not a score input; block rate, contested cells, hotspots and exit throughput are mandatory report diagnostics; a bottleneck is charged by its realized delay (Time against the cut-capacity bound Q_cut), exposure and losses (section 5.12)
Severe failures per quality component, a threshold in natural units; each severe component multiplies the score by 0.8 * (q / q_severe)
Normalization each quality component is a privileged relaxed optimum divided by the actual value, so it is relative to the difficulty of that exact episode
Failures missing, invalid output, model error and timeout score 0 and stay in every mean
Aggregation episode (mean over trials) -> scenario -> primary family -> overall, each an unweighted mean
Uncertainty stratified two-stage percentile bootstrap, 95%
Comparison paired; four outcomes; "better" needs the interval to exclude 0 and a difference of at least 1 point
Leaderboard score to 0.1, then safety-first tie-breaks; separate from significance
Environment one frozen environment pack per evaluation, identical for every model

2. Literature

Each source below is a primary source. The right column says what EMBERBench takes from it.

Source Relevant finding Use in EMBERBench
Mattson et al., MLPerf Training Benchmark, MLSys 2020, arXiv:1910.01500 A result counts only when the model reaches a fixed quality target; time-to-train is measured only after that gate; closed division rules fix the environment; multiple runs are required because of run-to-run variance. Safety acts as a gate and a multiplier, not as one more additive term. Fixed environment (pack). Repeated trials for stochastic models.

Helbing, Farkas and Vicsek, Simulating dynamical features of escape panic, Nature 2000 "Faster is slower": pushing at bottlenecks reduces the outflow. A bottleneck is charged by the outflow it loses: Time against a cut-capacity bound. The "jostler" adversary tests it.

Ouyang et al., An empirical study of the non-determinism of ChatGPT in code generation, ACM TOSEM 2024, arXiv:2308.02828 Outputs vary between identical requests even at temperature 0. Repeated trials; temperature 0 is not a determinism guarantee.

3. Design principles and how each is met

Principle Mechanism
Failure-sensitive multiplicative form; a zero component gives 0
Every component in [0, 1]; no bonus above 1 each component is a privileged bound over the actual value, or a probability-like factor
Safety dominates Safety multiplies Quality; an avoidable casualty caps the episode below 60; beta makes every run without loss and without a severe failure score above a perfect run with one casualty (R12)
Time and quality still matter below the thresholds Quality is continuous (Time 0.5 costs 22.5 points); severe failures cap the score below 80 (R2, R13, R14)
A casualty hurts more than a success helps incident factor 0.6 on the first casualty; the last evacuee adds at most 1 - (1 - 1/M*)^2
Multiple casualties progressively worse the incident factor is paid again at every doubling of 1 + c, and the rate term grows
Stranding and incomplete evacuation independent separate P_stranding and E
Trajectory invariance every input is a function of the replayed state trajectory, never of the action log alone (R16; the block-scrub adversary gains exactly 0)
Bottlenecks charged by their realized effect a severe realized bottleneck is a severe Time failure (T < 1/2 against Q_cut); its exposure and losses are in Hazard and Safety; blocked actions are diagnostics
Difficulty-relative time T = Q_cut / sum(A_i) with the exit- and cut-capacity bound over the privileged earliest escapes, so unavoidable queues at exits and doors are not charged; severe below 1/2
Exposure penalized without deaths H counts danger-zone states of evacuees above their privileged minimum; severe above 1/4
Bad routing penalized P against the fire-aware privileged minimum of moves
Failure to replan penalized R on route-invalidation events, plus the losses in Safety; severe below 1/2
Invalid output, model failure and timeout never disappear scheduled episodes without a result score 0 and are counted
No blind double counting Time, Hazard and Path use evacuees only, so losses are only in Safety; the blocked moves of people who later die are not an input; Path and Hazard are relative to fire-aware privileged minima, so necessary detours and unavoidable exposure are not charged; correlations checked (section 6.5)
Cost separate tokens, money and latency are never inputs; they are in a separate OperationalRecord

4. Metric classification

Metric Role Reason
evacuation rate core the primary outcome; normalized by M* so impossible rescues are not charged
casualties safety gate and core the most severe outcome
stranded safety gate and core a person still inside at the horizon is a failure, but less certain than a casualty
time to safety core speed matters once people are safe; evacuees only, so a death never "improves" time
congestion block rate diagnostic only, mandatory in every report an action-log count: replacing each blocked move with STAY gives the same trajectory with zero blocks, so it has no lower bound and is gameable at no cost (section 5.12)
fire-exposure proxy (sum 1/d) diagnostic it accumulates with time, so it double-counts speed (Spearman 0.87 with Time) and rewards reckless fast policies (section 5.5)
static path efficiency diagnostic it penalizes necessary fire detours (section 5.7)
replanning proxies diagnostic they react to any block, not to route invalidation by the fire
exit utilization / balance diagnostic only the right split depends on geometry and fire; rewarding balance would reward a 50/50 split when one exit is better. Allocation regret was considered: its cost is the extra time of a bad allocation, which T already measures against the allocation-optimal capacity bound Q_cut, so a separate term would double-count it
contested cells, hotspots, exit throughput diagnostic only, mandatory in every report contested cells and hotspots come from attempted moves (the block-scrub sets them to 0 on an identical trajectory); exit throughput is a trajectory property, but its cost is the delay already in T
decision cost operational only not a property of evacuation quality

5. Candidate formulas and why they were rejected

Every candidate was scored on the same evidence (section 6). The full tables are in scoring-study-results.md.

5.1 Additive weighted sum

100 * sum(w_k * x_k) over evacuation, survival, stranding and the quality components. Rejected. One casualty in 100 scores 99.4 (R1 fails), 10 casualties in 100 score 94.4, and 50% losses still score above 0. The "abandon the farthest 15%" adversary is only 7.2 points below congestion-aware. A weighted sum lets speed and order buy back lives, which violates the Safety Gym and constrained-MDP view.

5.2 Plain product of rates

100 * (evacuated / N) * (1 - c / N) * .... Rejected. One casualty in 100 scores 99.0. A product of rates is failure-sensitive only when a rate is near 0, so isolated deaths in large crowds are almost free. It also fails R5, R6, R7 and R9.

5.3 Official form without the incident factor (alpha = 1)

Rejected. One casualty in 100 scores 94.1 and one stranded person 96.1, so a run with a death keeps a "95-level" score. R1, R5, R6 and R7 fail. The rate term alone scales with the population; the discrete incident factor is needed so that the first loss is expensive in any crowd.

5.4 Arithmetic (compensatory) quality mean

Rejected. Time 0.1 with everything else perfect scores 73 (R10 fails): a very slow evacuation is hidden by perfect coordination and path. The geometric mean keeps each component's failure visible, as Fleming and Wallace recommend for normalized ratios: it gives 42 alone (design A, section 5.9), and 8.5 with the severe gate of the official form.

5.5 Dose-based hazard (sum 1/d relative to the privileged minimum dose)

Rejected. The first study run used the metrics exposure proxy. It has Spearman 0.87 with Time, so it counts speed twice. It also rewarded the reckless fire-ignoring adversary: Hazard 69.1 against 64.5 for congestion-aware, because a fast run accumulates less dose even when it passes close to the fire. The replacement is the avoidable danger-zone rate: states within 2 cells of fire above the privileged minimum, divided by the evacuee states. Its Spearman correlation with Time is 0.14, and reckless now scores below congestion-aware on Hazard (89.4 against 89.9).

5.6 Horizon-normalized time (W - A) / (W - L)

W is the horizon, A the actual and L the lower bound. Rejected. It depends on the scenario horizon and compresses differences: with H = 200, L = 20 and A = 40 it gives 0.89, while the ratio L / A gives 0.5. A long horizon would make slow runs look good. Baseline normalization (A - random) / (best - random) was rejected for the same reason: the reference baseline changes the scale, and random scores 0 on almost every scenario. The degenerate cases of L / A are handled: L >= 1 for every evacuee (an exit is never a start cell), and with no evacuee Time is undefined, not 1.

5.7 Static path efficiency

The metrics path efficiency uses the wall-only distance. Rejected as a score input: it charges a policy for a detour that the fire made necessary (first study: fire-aware 95.3 against static 100). The replacement bound is the privileged fire-aware minimum number of moves. It reduces that unfairness (fire-aware 97.3); the rest is the extra distance of the fire-aware baseline over the privileged optimum. Path has Spearman 0.48 with Time and the smallest weight (0.1).

5.8 Replanning set to 1 when nothing was invalidated

Rejected. It would fabricate a perfect component and raise the scores of easy scenarios. Replanning is undefined there and its weight is shared by the other components.

5.9 How Safety dominates: designs A to F

The first version 1.0 draft was 100 * Safety * Quality (design A). It has a safety ceiling (one casualty: at most 60) but not safety dominance: a safe run with Time below about 0.22 scored below a perfect run with one casualty in a large crowd. A pure lexicographic order fixes that but makes Time irrelevant. Six designs were compared on the same evidence and the same requirements (section 6.2, R1 to R15) and pre-registered orderings (section 6.3):

Design Definition Fails Decision
A multiplicative (draft) 100 * Safety * Quality R12, R14 rejected: a severe exposure (rate 0.3) still scores 89; Time 0.1 still scores 32
B hard ceilings A, capped at 80 when any component is severe R12, R14 rejected: a flat cap does not grow with severity, and A's dominance gap remains
C safety tiers Quality interpolates between this Safety level and the next worse one R10, R13, R14 rejected: Quality moves the score too little; Time 0.1 scores 70
D lexicographic (epsilon 0.05) 100 * Safety * (1 - 0.05 * (1 - Quality)) R2, R8, R10, R13, R14 rejected: Time becomes irrelevant (Time 0.1 scores 97)
E soft minimum weighted power mean (p = -5) of Safety and the quality components R1, R11, R12, R14 rejected: one casualty scores 63, and a severe exposure (rate 0.3) still scores 90
F constrained geometric 100 * Safety * Quality^beta * Severity none adopted

Two variants of F were also checked. With the dominance floor set at the stranding factor 0.8 instead of the casualty factor 0.6 (beta = 0.32), Quality is flatter: Time 0.5 costs 10.6 points instead of 22.5, and a severe Time 0.4 scores 55 instead of 46. It meets R1 to R16 with the current weights, but only just (R2 asks for 10 points). It is not adopted: the official beta is the largest exponent for which R12 holds by construction (q_min ^ beta >= alpha_c), so it gives Quality the most influence that safety dominance allows; any smaller exponent is an arbitrary flattening. Plain products and additive forms fail the safety requirements (sections 5.1 and 5.2).

Why F works:

  • beta = ln(0.6) / ln(0.5) is derived from the casualty incident factor and the smallest severe threshold. Every run whose quality components are all at or above their thresholds has Quality^beta >= 0.6, which is above the Safety of any run with an avoidable casualty. So R12 holds by construction, in every population, not only in the tested cases.
  • Below the thresholds, Quality is continuous and still costs points: Time 0.5 costs 22.5 points.
  • At a threshold, the severe factor 0.8 * (q / q_severe) adds a step and a slope. A severe failure caps the score below 80 and keeps falling with severity, so a severe realized bottleneck, delay, exposure or replanning failure is a substantial, permanent loss (R13, R14).

5.10 Aggregate attribution of unavoidable losses

The draft charged unavoidable losses to casualties first, from counts: c = max(0, C - U) with U = N - M*. Rejected. It transfers an "allowance" between people. If person a could escape but dies, and person b could not escape and is stranded, the draft gives c = 0: the avoidable death disappears. The official rule is per person (scoring.md, section 4.1). On the study episodes the two rules happen to agree (0 of 1248 episodes differ), but test_real_episode_person_level_attribution builds a real replayed episode where the draft hides a casualty. R11 checks the same case on synthetic evidence.

5.11 Exit balance and allocation regret

A 50/50 exit balance is never a goal and is not scored. An allocation regret term (extra time over an allocation-optimal schedule) was considered. Not adopted as a separate term: the time cost of a bad allocation is already in T, because T compares with the capacity bound Q_cut, which is allocation-optimal at the exits and at every door-like cut. A separate term would charge the same seconds twice. Exit utilization stays a diagnostic.

5.12 Coordination: the metric contract

Scoring 1.0 at commit ce5e322 had a Coordination component C = (1 - block rate)^2 (weight 0.2) and a severe gate at a block rate above 1/4. This section tests whether any Coordination component can be measured without rewarding a different action encoding of the same realized trajectory. Full tables: scoring-study-results.md.

Invariant (adopted, R16). If two policies produce byte-identical authenticated state trajectories (positions, statuses and fire at every step), their core EMBER Score is identical. It is desirable because the benchmark claims to measure evacuation outcomes: the people, the fire and the timing are the same, so nothing the score is meant to measure differs. Only the action log differs, and the action log is an encoding choice.

Block-scrub reproduction. The adversary adv-block-scrub runs congestion-aware-v1, predicts the public resolver, and replaces every move that would be blocked by a person (occupied, conflict, cycle) with STAY.

Suite Episodes Identical state trajectories Identical trajectory evidence Blocks (congestion-aware) Blocks (scrub)
public 30 30 30 4449 0
generated 48 48 48 844 0
held-out 48 48 48 1211 0

Every observation, state, fire set, evacuation, casualty, stranding, person-time, path and danger-zone count is identical; only the action labels, the movement outcomes, the block counts and the action-based crowd counts (contested cells, hotspots) differ. Under the 1.0 score the scrub gains +4.5 points on the public suite, +7.4 on the generated suite, +5.2 on both and +3.1 on the held-out suite, and up to 70.9 points in one episode (the block-rate gate). test_block_scrub_keeps_trajectory_and_score is the permanent regression test.

The concepts. "Coordination" mixes eight things:

Concept What it is Trajectory property? In the score?
A attempted coordination / action intent which moves a policy asks for no (action log) no: the scrub changes it without changing the outcome
B realized coordination outcome who leaves when, and who survives yes yes, as Time, Hazard and Safety
C unavoidable capacity congestion queues that every policy must form at exits and doors yes no charge: inside the bound Q_cut
D avoidable congestion queues a policy causes, for example everyone at one exit yes yes, as Time above Q_cut (and Hazard while waiting)
E queueing delay waiting person-steps yes yes, once: it is part of person-time, so in Time
F exit / cut throughput loss fewer evacuations per open slot than possible yes yes, once: lost throughput is extra person-time, so in Time; throughput is reported
G conflicts that change the trajectory a contest that moves a person later yes, through its effect yes, through its effect on Time, Hazard, Safety
H conflicts that do not change the trajectory a blocked move where STAY gives the same state no no

So the score measures B to G through their realized effects, and A and H not at all. Blocked moves are not all bad: in a door queue that no policy can avoid, a move into the queue is blocked or not depending only on the resolver's order, and the evacuation is optimal either way.

Why blocks cannot be scored.

  1. No lower bound. Each other component is a privileged lower bound over the actual value. The block count has no positive lower bound: the scrub reaches 0 on every trajectory, so "bound / actual" does not exist.
  2. Gameable at no cost. The resolver is public and pure, and its inputs are all in the observation. Any agent can scrub, with no change in outcome.
  3. Not defensible as decision quality. Keeping an action-based component would need the statement "a policy that predicts a blocked move and emits STAY is genuinely better than a policy that attempts it, even when both produce the same trajectory". It is not defended here: predicting the resolver's tie-breaks is knowledge of the simulator, not evacuation skill, and it changes no person's fate.
  4. Dead-person double charge. On the generated study suite, 598 of the 844 blocks of congestion-aware-v1 belong to people who end as casualties or stranded; their losses are already in Safety. G6/G7 remove these blocks, but the remaining blocks still fail points 1 to 3.

Candidates. Each candidate is scored on the same evidence (study suites and held-out suite). G5-official is the adopted form.

All candidates meet R1 to R15 in their restated form except T-wait. The 1.0 statements of R8 and R13 in action-log units ("a block rate of 0.3 alone costs 10 points") are withdrawn: G4 and G5 fail them by design, because a block rate without delay is not a failure of any outcome.

  • Overlap with Time. Spearman over 1064 moving-policy episodes: Time and the 1.0 Coordination 0.14, Time and the G7 Coordination 0.67, Time and T-wait 0.88. The 1.0 component carries information that Time does not, but the scrub shows that this information is the action encoding. The more a block measure is cleaned of encoding (G7), the more it becomes Time.
  • Overlap with Safety. 1.0 and G1 count the blocks of people who die (the dead-person double charge). G6, G7 and G5 do not.
  • Interpretability. G5 is the simplest: delay is in one place, and the block rate is a reported explanation. G7 needs the cut bound, per-person block attribution and a credit rule, and is still not invariant.
  • Cost. G1, G4, G5 and G7 need the cut bound (one BFS per threshold and one forward reachability per evacuee, well below a second per episode on the study suites). G6 and G7 also need per-person action attribution.
  • Generated suite. Under 1.0 the generated suite alone ranked reckless-fast (31.4) and static-shortest (28.1) above congestion-aware (26.0), through the block-rate gate. Under G5, congestion-aware is first on the generated suite (33.1), on the public suite (53.2) and on the held-out suite (40.5). This was not the selection criterion: G7 also ranks congestion-aware second only behind its own scrub, and is rejected for R16.

A trajectory-only Coordination (attempted). The natural trajectory-only definition is the avoidable waiting of evacuees: waiting person-steps above the capacity-forced delay, (T - m) - (Q_cut - L), where T is person-time, m cells moved, Q_cut the capacity bound and L the free-flow bound. Every term is a Time or Path input, so the measure is an algebraic combination of the Time and Path components, and it correlates 0.88 with Time. Excess person-time over Q_cut is T - Q_cut, a monotone transform of Time itself. Density-based measures (occupied neighbours, occupancy near cuts) have no privileged lower bound that separates necessary crowding at an exit from avoidable crowding, so they cannot be normalized per episode like the other components. No trajectory-based, non-redundant Coordination component exists under V1 semantics.

Decision: D, remove Coordination from the headline score for V1. The quality weights are renormalized over the remaining components in their old proportions (Time 3/8, Hazard 2/8, Path 1/8, Replanning 2/8). Time uses the cut-capacity bound Q_cut (scoring.md, section 4.2), so unavoidable queues at exits and doors are not charged and avoidable queues are. A severe realized bottleneck is a severe Time failure. The congestion diagnostics are mandatory in every report (ScoreReport.diagnostics). A future scoring version can add a Coordination component only with a privileged lower bound on a trajectory property that is not a function of Time.

6. Sensitivity study

6.1 Setup

python tools/scoring_sensitivity.py plays 16 policies on three suites:

  • the public V1 suite with the public development seeds 0, 1, 2 (30 episodes),
  • a generated stochastic study suite: 16 scenarios on a 16x12 grid with 6 to 24 people, stochastic fire and a horizon of 80, from a public config and a constant test-only generator seed (20261020), with seeds 0, 1, 2 (48 episodes), and
  • a held-out generated suite from the same config and a fresh, pre-registered generator seed (20261021), with seeds 0, 1, 2 (48 episodes). It is used only for validation (section 6.10), never to choose a constant or a candidate.

The public and generated suites together are the study suites.

Policies:

  • reference baselines: always-stay, random-legal-v1, nearest-exit-greedy-v1, static-shortest-path-v1, fire-aware-shortest-path-v1, congestion-aware-v1;
  • adversaries built to game a score: reckless-fast (load-balanced shortest paths that ignore the fire), abandon-far (the 15% farthest people stay), safe-slow (moves every third step unless the fire is close), late-start (waits 8 steps), jostler (prefers occupied cells), herd (everyone to one exit), wanderer (sideways moves when far from the fire), hider (runs from the fire instead of to an exit unless an exit is close), fire-hugger (congestion-aware routing that prefers cells next to the fire) and block-scrub (congestion-aware with every person-blocked move replaced by STAY; section 5.12).

The privileged bound is a reference, not a policy. All 2016 episodes (16 policies x 126 episodes; 1248 in the study suites) are scored with person-level attribution.

6.2 Requirements

A candidate is acceptable only if it meets all of these on hand-made evidence. They were written before the candidates were scored, and they pin the thresholds normatively: R2 says that Time must matter, R13 and R14 say what a severe failure must cost. R8 and R13 are stated in realized units: a bottleneck is described by the delay it causes against the capacity bound, not by blocked actions (section 5.12). R16 was added by the coordination contract.

ID Requirement

Results: the official form (F, G5) passes all 16. The other designs fail as listed in sections 5.9 and 5.12.

6.7 Constant grids and rank stability

Two grids vary the constants around the official values. For each combination the study checks R1 to R14 and the Kendall tau of the policy ranking against the official ranking. Tau is reported for every combination, feasible or not, so the ranking is not a selection criterion.

Safety grid (81 combinations): alpha_c in {0.5, 0.6, 0.7}, k_c in {2, 4, 6}, kE in {1, 2, 3}, alpha_s in {0.7, 0.8, 0.9}.

  • 26 of 81 meet every requirement; the official constants are feasible.
  • Failures: R1 in 27 (all with alpha_c = 0.7, one casualty above 60), R6 in 30, R11 in 27, R4 in 15, R7 in 9.
  • Every combination: tau min 0.983, mean 0.996, largest score change 13.2 points. Feasible combinations: tau min 0.983, mean 0.996, largest change 8.4 points.

Gate grid (81 combinations): phi in {0.7, 0.8, 0.9}, gamma in {0.5, 1, 2}, severe Time in {1/3, 1/2, 2/3}, severe hazard rate in {1/5, 1/4, 1/3}. The quality exponent beta is re-derived for each combination, and R12 is checked at that combination's own threshold corner. (The 1.0 grid also varied the severe block rate; that threshold no longer exists.)

  • 36 of 81 meet every requirement; the official gates are feasible.
  • Failures: R13 in 27 (all with severe Time 1/3: Time 0.4 is then not severe), R14 in 45 (severe Time 1/3 or hazard rate 1/3).
  • Every combination: tau min 0.898, mean 0.971, largest score change 6.9 points. Feasible combinations: tau min 0.983, mean 0.999, largest change 6.8 points.
  • The official thresholds (Time 1/2, hazard rate 1/4) are the most lenient grid values that still meet R13 and R14. Stricter values also pass, but mark more episodes as severe. So the thresholds are pinned by the requirements, not by the ranking. phi and gamma are not pinned: all three values of each pass.

The severe Path and Replanning thresholds (1/2) are not in the grid. They follow the same rule as Time: twice the privileged need is a failure.

6.8 Weight perturbation

Each quality weight multiplied by 0.5 and by 1.5, then renormalized: Kendall tau is 1.000 in every case and no policy moves by more than 1.3 points. The ranking does not depend on the exact weights.

6.9 Agreement between designs

Kendall tau of each design's ranking against the official one: A 0.983, B 0.983, C 0.949, D 0.915, E 1.000, F with the stranding floor 0.966, additive 0.831, plain product 0.898. The multiplicative designs rank the policies almost alike. They differ in the requirements, which is why the requirements, not the ranking, select the design.

6.10 Held-out validation

The held-out suite (generator seed 20261021, 48 episodes per policy) was generated after every decision and checked once.

  • Baseline order: congestion-aware 40.5 > fire-aware 37.1 > static 33.2 > greedy 9.3 > random 0.0 = stay 0.0, the same order as on the study suites.
  • Kendall tau of the 16-policy ranking, study suites against held-out: 0.814. The changes are among adversaries and greedy: greedy falls to 9.3 (it stalls in the held-out high-density layouts), reckless-fast falls from 4th to 7th, and abandon-far rises from 12th to 9th.
  • Block-scrub: 48 of 48 identical state trajectories, 1211 blocks against 0, score delta exactly 0 (R16). Under the 1.0 score the scrub would gain +3.1 points there.
  • Cut-capacity bound: Q_cut <= person-time in every one of the 1773 episodes with evacuees (all suites, all policies); Q_cut > Q_exit in 104 generated and 60 held-out episodes, never on the public suite.
  • Severe gates (any): congestion-aware 12.5%, fire-aware 16.7%, static 12.5%; Time is never severe for a moving baseline.

8. Statistical rule

  • Interval. Stratified two-stage percentile bootstrap, 95%, 2000 resamples, deterministic SHAKE-256 stream (scoring.md, section 7).
  • Comparison. Paired by episode. a_better needs the interval of the paired difference to exclude 0 and a difference of at least 1 point. Otherwise inconclusive, unless every paired score is identical (exact_tie).
  • Leaderboard. Order by the score to 0.1, then Safety, avoidable casualties, avoidable stranded, worst scenario, model ID. Order is not significance; publish both.
  • Interpretation of the interval. It describes this sample of scenarios and seeds. With one scenario per family it cannot see scenario variance. Claims about unseen environments need held-out generated suites with several scenarios per family.

9. Limitations

  • The references are relaxations. Path and the release steps of the Time bound ignore other people. The Time bound Q_cut adds exit capacity and the capacity of each wall-distance cut, one cut at a time; it ignores interactions between successive cuts and the flow inside rooms. So Time and Path can stay below 1 even for an optimal crowd policy in a dense scenario. This makes the scale conservative, not biased between models. Q_cut is proved to be a lower bound (scoring.md, section 4.2) and is never above the realized person-time in any study or held-out episode.
  • Hazard uses a fixed radius of 2 cells and ignores walls in the distance, like the metrics proxy.
  • With one scenario per family the interval reflects only seed and trial variance (section 8).
  • The constants are chosen by explicit requirements and checked for rank stability; other reasonable requirements would give other constants. The study and the requirements are published so that they can be challenged.
  • The score is not purely lexicographic in safety, on purpose. A safe run can score below a perfect run with one casualty only when the safe run itself has a severe quality failure (R12 and scoring.md, section 4.3).
  • Congestion is scored only through its realized effects (section 5.12). A policy that causes many blocked moves but no extra delay, exposure or loss is not charged, by design (R16). Coordination quality beyond those effects is not measured by V1; a future component needs a privileged lower bound on a trajectory property that is not a function of Time.
  • The severe thresholds are normative choices pinned by R13 and R14. The grids show which values meet them (section 6.7), but other reasonable requirements would give other thresholds.
