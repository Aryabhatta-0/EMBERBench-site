Difficulty bands and calibration (research, V1)

Field Value
Status Research design. Not merged. Uses the official EMBER Score (scoring version 1.0).
Code Library since Phase 11: CALIBRATION_PANEL, measure_world, calibrate, difficulty_band, capacity_savable, capacity_ceiling, fire_evidence in emberbench.calibration (calibration panel 1.0, difficulty rules 1.0, admission rules 1.0); family_tags in emberbench.families; select_suite and the pool workflow in emberbench.composition. The research tool tools/analyze_suite_design.py imports them. See generator.md.
Evidence suite-design-results.md (development pool: 1,120 generated scenarios, 2,320 unique worlds)
Score used The official EMBER Score from emberbench.scoring (score_episode, score_evidence). The tool has no other score.

This document defines how a scenario gets an admission decision and a difficulty band, and how the evaluator uses the bands to compose the unseen suite. The parent design is evaluation-suite-design.md.

1. Requirements

  1. A band is computed from measurements. Nobody labels a scenario by hand.
  2. The computation is deterministic and reproducible from the scenario, its unique worlds and the frozen calibration panel.
  3. No model under evaluation is an input. The rule is not fitted to any model.
  4. The rule is calibrated on development worlds (public development seeds) only. Hidden worlds are only measured, evaluator-side, with the rule frozen beforehand.
  5. The composed suite separates methods. It must not be mostly worlds where every reference policy scores about 100, or about 0.

2. The frozen calibration panel (version 1.0)

Role Policy Use
Panel member nearest-exit-greedy-v1 band input
Panel member static-shortest-path-v1 band input
Panel member fire-aware-shortest-path-v1 band input
Panel member congestion-aware-v1 band input
Floor reference random-legal-v1 reported only (the analogue of Procgen's R_min); it scores 0 on every public world
Privileged reference M* (official max_evacuated), M_cap (section 6), official time and path bounds a bound only. It normalizes the official score and excludes capacity artifacts. It never sets a band.

The panel is the four public, non-random V1 baselines, in the order of increasing information use. Its members, its version (CALIBRATION_PANEL_VERSION = "1.0") and the band thresholds are frozen and recorded in the freeze record of every suite. A change to any of them is a new panel version and needs new suites.

No circular calibration.

  • The panel is fixed before any model is evaluated. A model under evaluation is never a panel member and never an input to admission, banding or selection (tests check the signatures).
  • The thresholds are fixed numbers with a stated meaning on the official score scale (section 4). They are not quantiles of a pool and not fitted to make any policy look good.
  • The panel members define the bands, so their own scores on a selected suite are biased by the selection. The suite-design study therefore measures rank stability also with two comparators that are not panel members: a congestion-aware policy with an ε share of uniformly random legal actions, ε = 0.05 and ε = 0.2.
  • fire-aware-shortest-path-v1 and congestion-aware-v1 are practically tied on the pool (mean 61.1 and 61.6). The design study does not count their order as evidence.

3. Evidence: structural proxies do not predict difficulty

Rank correlation (Spearman) between each proxy and 100 - B over the admitted development scenarios, where B is the best panel score (section 4):

Proxy rho
population 0.35
crowd density 0.17
N / min cut 0.20
obstacle density -0.48
route redundancy 0.34
ignition-person distance -0.30
threatened share (privileged) 0.27
recoverable invalidation (privileged) 0.57
savable share M*/N (privileged) 0.04
  • No structural proxy reaches |rho| = 0.5. Obstacle density correlates negatively: mazes slow the fire and are easy for any planner. A Safety-Gym-style level ("more obstacles is harder") would be wrong here.
  • The best single predictor is privileged: the share of savable people whose every static shortest route the fire cuts (0.57). It needs the seed, so it is evaluator-side.
  • Proxies are therefore used for family tags and quality checks, not for bands.

4. Band definition

For a scenario, take each panel member's mean official EMBER Score over the scenario's applicable unique worlds (worlds with M* > 0). B is the highest of the four means.

Band Rule (official points) Best panel member, development pool (median avoidable losses)
Easy B >= 80 0
Moderate 30 <= B < 80 1
Hard 10 <= B < 30 1.75
Stress B < 10 3

Why these cutpoints. The official score is steep in avoidable losses. With perfect quality, one avoidable loss gives at most 52.5 when M* = 10, 65.2 when M* = 20, 76.8 when M* = 100; two avoidable losses out of 10 give 28.8, three give 15.4 (computed with score_evidence). So:

  • B >= 80 means that the best fair heuristic has no avoidable loss and a good quality. On the pool, the mean avoidable loss of the best member in the Easy band is 0.01.
  • 30 <= B < 80 means about one avoidable loss (or none with a severe quality failure).
  • 10 <= B < 30 means about two avoidable losses.
  • B < 10 means three or more: the best V1 heuristic fails badly. This band is the headroom against saturation.

The earlier proposal (before PR #32) used worst >= 90 and best >= 90 on a safety-only analysis score. On the official score, the old Easy rule worst >= 90 mostly selects saturated worlds (every panel member is at 95 or more on 28.6% of the pool worlds), which carry no ranking information. The rule is replaced by the B rule above and an explicit saturation exclusion (section 5).

Why B, not the mean or the worst member. A band states how much of the attainable score the best fair V1 heuristic keeps: the regret of the strongest available reference, as in PAIRED (Dennis et al., 2020). The spread of the panel is used for admission (section 5), not for the band.

5. Admission rule (deterministic, in this order)

Order Exclusion Rule Pool share
1 not_applicable no world has a savable person (M* = 0); the official score is NOT_APPLICABLE 0.9%
2 capacity_artifact mean capacity ceiling < 50 (section 6) 4.1%
3 saturated every panel member >= 95 24.1%
4 floor every panel member < 1 2.2%
5 no_separation panel spread B - worst < the official practical difference (1 point) 12.9%
— admitted every other scenario; banded by B 55.7%

A saturated or floor scenario carries no ranking information for V1-level policies. A no-separation scenario cannot rank the panel itself. All three still count in the pool statistics, so the acceptance rates of section 7 are honest.

6. Practical unsolvability: the capacity filter (M_cap)

The official M* counts the people who can escape alone against the precomputed fire. It ignores that people share exits and cuts. In a dense single-exit room, M* can be 35 while at most 14 people can physically leave before the fire closes the exit. Every policy then "loses" 21 savable people, and the official score is 0 for every policy: the world measures the bound, not the policy.

capacity_savable(scenario, seed) returns (M*, M_cap). M_cap <= M* adds the two capacity facts behind the official time bound Q_cut:

  • at most one person leaves through an open exit cell per step;
  • at most k_s people stand on the cut N(C) of a component C of the cells with wall distance > d at step s (the same cut groups as the official _cut_bound).

Each savable person gets an interval of feasible steps per exit and per cut. The maximum number of people who fit is an interval scheduling problem, solved exactly by Glover's earliest-deadline greedy. M_cap is still an upper bound (the relaxation ignores movement conflicts), so it is safe to use as a filter.

capacity_ceiling(N, M*, M_cap) is the best official score that a perfect policy can reach when M* - M_cap savable people are capacity-limited: score_evidence on synthetic evidence with M* - M_cap avoidable stranded people and perfect quality. It uses the official formula; the tool has no second implementation.

Rule: a scenario whose mean capacity ceiling is below CEILING_MIN = 50 is excluded as a capacity artifact. The official score is not changed: M* stays the official reference. The filter only stops such scenarios from entering a suite.

Measurement (development pool) Value
applicable worlds with M_cap < M* 92 of 2,286 (4.0%)
scenarios with such a world 75
scenarios excluded (ceiling < 50) 46 (41 of them would otherwise be Stress)
panel runs that evacuated more than M_cap 0 (2,320 worlds x 4 members): the bound holds
capacity-limited worlds where the best member reaches M_cap exactly 50: the bound is tight there
public V1 suite M_cap = M* on all 73 unique worlds

The artifacts come mostly from the dense-single recipe (34 of its 120 scenarios). Without the filter they would enter the Stress band, where every policy scores about 0 for a reason that no policy controls.

  • The bands are ordered for every comparator, including the two non-panel comparators. The calibration is not circular in effect either.
  • The Easy band still separates policies strongly (nearest-exit-greedy 14.2 against congestion-aware 94.0): "Easy" means easy for an informed planner, not for every policy.
  • The official score is bimodal on single scenarios (histogram of B: 224 of 1,110 applicable scenarios below 10, 595 at 90 or more). The Moderate band is the scarcest (7.9% of candidates).

Public V1 suite, same rule (one world per deterministic scenario, 64 worlds for the stochastic one): pub-01 and pub-04 saturated; pub-03 and pub-08 Easy; pub-02 and pub-10 Moderate; pub-06, pub-07 and pub-09 Hard; pub-05 Stress.

8. Composition rule

  1. Every candidate gets its calibrate result and its family tags (family_tags, fixed thresholds on the structural profile and on the evaluator-side fire profile).
  2. select_suite(candidates, quotas, selection_key) fills the family x band quotas. Families are filled scarcest first; each candidate is used once and its selected family becomes its primary family; inside a cell the order is a keyed SHA-256 of the scenario content hash, so the private key, not the candidate order or a model, decides which candidate is taken.
  3. A cell that cannot be filled is reported as a shortfall. It is never filled from another band in silence. The evaluator generates more candidates for that cell (targeted recipe), or the freeze record states the shortfall.
  4. The fire-model quota (stochastic slots per family) is applied in the same way, as part of the cell key.

Phase 11 implements this rule as emberbench.composition.select_suite (selection rules 1.0). Inside a family the bands are filled Easy..Stress and the stochastic cell of a band before its deterministic cell. The stochastic slots follow the PR #33 even-spread rule: Moderate 1, Hard 2, Stress 1 per family. Calibration and fire tags of a stochastic candidate are measured on exactly the 3 unique worlds that build_unique_world_manifest schedules for it. The targeted top-up of step 3 is compose_pool with the V1_TOP_UP recipe table (generator.md, section 5).

9. Known limits

  • Bands are relative to V1 heuristics. A strong model can find Stress worlds solvable. That is the intended headroom; saturation triggers a new panel version (evaluation-suite-design.md, section 16).
  • M_cap is a relaxation. Some admitted Stress worlds can still be harder than any policy can solve. The filter removes the clear capacity artifacts only.
  • Family tags use fixed thresholds on measured properties. Since generator 1.1.0, describe_families uses the same rules (family rules 1.0) for its structural labels; the two fire-dependent families stay evaluator-side. The evaluator-side tags are the stratification key.
  • The noisy comparators share their base policy with a panel member. They test rank stability; they are not independent agents.

10. Reproducibility and tests

Every band and admission decision is a pure function of the scenario, its unique worlds (by environment_world_digest) and the frozen panel. The tool caches expensive runs outside the repository, keyed by every version; the reported numbers are recomputed from the final code. tests/test_suite_design.py checks the band thresholds, the admission order, the capacity bound on a scenario with M_cap < M*, Glover's greedy, the capacity ceiling against the official score, the official scoring of panel runs and the selector rules. tests/test_calibration.py checks the library rules, and tests/test_composition.py checks the composition pipeline.
